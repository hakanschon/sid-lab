# Sound design and influences

All 104 factory presets were created for SID Lab. No commercial preset bank or recorded artist performance was imported. They use the same visible 64-parameter sound schema available to users and are embedded in the instrument; matching JSON exports are in [`presets/Factory/`](../presets/Factory/).

The disco bank explores Moroder-style movement with varied envelopes, waveforms, register, filtering and oscillator relationships. Munich Midnight is a rounded bass; Neon Autobahn a dry pulse; Transistor Nights a nasal band-pass tone; Disco Databus a brass pluck; Glass Transmission a metallic mallet; Silver Boulevard a string texture. Sequencer patterns are original and remain separate from the underlying timbre.

The 80s bank takes inspiration from Alphaville's first-album synth textures. Japan Fifth Bass layers oscillators a fifth apart; Melody String Machine and the eight later Melody voices explore strings, staccato chords, bright leads, pulses and soft backdrops. These are SID interpretations, not exact reproductions of the original studio equipment, performances or orchestrations. “Sounds Like a Melody” also used orchestral strings; those recordings are not included.

The eight kits are variations of three SID recipe families. They provide kicks, snares, hats, toms and metallic percussion using actual SID waveforms/envelopes. MIDI notes follow the documented GM/fallback layout, and one-shot tails survive short note-offs. They are not samples of a named commercial drum machine.

For musical context, see [Giorgio Moroder's discussion of Daft Punk's recording](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/recording-random-access-memories-daft-punk), [Marian Gold on “Big in Japan”](https://www.songwritingmagazine.co.uk/songs/how-i-wrote/big-in-japan-alphaville), and [SWR's account of “Sounds Like a Melody”](https://www.swr.de/kultur/musik/hits-und-storys-alphaville-sounds-like-a-melody-100.html). Artist names describe inspiration and imply no affiliation or endorsement.
